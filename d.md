Here is your prioritized **"Do or Die" Exam Cheat Sheet**. I have filtered the content into **High Priority** (Must do for passing) and **Low/Medium Priority** (Read if you have time).

### **Unit 1: Fundamentals**

#### **2 Marks (Short)**

**Top 3 Very Important (Memorize These):**

1.  **What is a Data Structure?** Definition and 2 applications (Compiler, DBMS).

2.  **Primitive vs. Non-Primitive:** Basic types (int) vs. Derived types (Array/List).

3.  **Time Complexity:** Definition (Time taken vs. Input size).

- **Less Important:** Sparse Matrix , Abstract Data Types (ADT) , Pointers & Arrays.

#### **10 Marks (Long)**

**Top 2 High Priority (Sure-Shot):**

1. **Trace Binary Search:** Find 37 in the list `5, 12...62`. Show Low, Mid, High calculations.

2. **Quicksort:** Explain the logic (Pivot, Partition, Recursion).

- **Medium/Low Priority:** Linear Search Program , Constructors & Destructors , Classes & Objects.

---

### **Unit 2: Stacks & Queues**

#### **2 Marks (Short)**

**Top 3 Very Important (Memorize These):**

1.  **Difference between Stack & Queue:** LIFO vs FIFO, Top vs Front/Rear.

2.  **Postfix Conversion:** Convert to .

3.  **Overflow & Underflow:** Push to Full / Pop from Empty.

- **Less Important:** Define Stack , Applications of Stack , Define Deque.

#### **10 Marks (Long)**

**Top 2 High Priority (Sure-Shot):**

1.  **Postfix Evaluation Algorithm:** Write the steps (Push operand, Pop operator) and trace an example.

2.  **Stack ADT:** Explain Push and Pop algorithms with Overflow/Underflow checks.

- **Medium/Low Priority:** Queue Program , Infix/Prefix/Postfix Definitions , Applications of Queues.

---

### **Unit 3: Linked Lists**

#### **2 Marks (Short)**

**Top 3 Very Important (Memorize These):**

1.  **Advantage over Arrays:** Dynamic size, easy insertion/deletion.

2.  **Delete a Node:** Logic to delete '25' from `12->7->25->3` (Update 7's next to 3).

3.  **Header Node:** Definition (Dummy node at start) and use.

- **Less Important:** C++ Node Creation , Polynomial Representation , Next vs Prev Pointer.

#### **10 Marks (Long)**

**Top 2 High Priority (Sure-Shot):**

1.  **Insertion & Deletion:** Explain with **Diagrams** (drawing nodes and arrows is key).

2.  **Polynomial Addition:** Algorithm to add two polynomials.

- **Medium/Low Priority:** Compare SLL, DLL, CLL , Stack/Queue using Linked List , Real-life applications.

---

### **Unit 4: Trees**

#### **2 Marks (Short)**

**Top 3 Very Important (Memorize These):**

1.  **In-Order Traversal:** Left Root Right (Output for 10, 5, 15 is 5, 10, 15).

2.  **Strictly Binary Tree:** Every node has 0 or 2 children.

3.  **AVL Balance Factor:** Height(Left) - Height(Right). Range: -1, 0, 1.

- **Less Important:** Leaf Node Definition , Huffman Optimality , B-Tree vs B+ Tree.

#### **10 Marks (Long)**

**Top 2 High Priority (Sure-Shot):**

1.  **BST Traversal Code:** Write In-order/Post-order output for the given tree diagram.

2.  **Huffman Tree:** Construct tree for A:5, B:9... and generate codes.

- **Medium/Low Priority:** B+ Tree Structure , AVL Rotations , Applications of Trees.

---

### **Unit 5: Graphs**

#### **2 Marks (Short)**

**Top 3 Very Important (Memorize These):**

1.  **Matrix vs. List:** Adjacency Matrix (Fast, High Memory) vs Adjacency List (Slow, Low Memory).

2.  **Tree vs. Graph:** Tree has no cycles; Graph can have cycles.

3.  **Topological Sort Application:** Task Scheduling.

- **Less Important:** Path/Degree/Cycle , Directed vs Undirected , Drawing a simple graph.

#### **10 Marks (Long)**

**Top 2 High Priority (Sure-Shot):**

1.  **DFS or BFS Traversal:** Perform traversal on the graph provided in the paper (A to Z order).

2.  **Topological Sort (Kahn's):** Sort the DAG using In-Degree method.

- **Medium/Low Priority:** Bipartite Graphs , Applications of Graphs.
